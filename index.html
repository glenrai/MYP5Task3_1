You live in the world of M. T. Anderson's Feed. Your friend's feed was damaged during a hacker attack, and the corporation that makes the feed has refused to repair it because her shopping profile does not make her a valuable customer. Create a letter to the Chief Executive of FeedTech Corporation persuading the company to repair your friend's feed and explaining why it has a responsibility to do so.
(Approximately 350 words)

Before you write: context and register

ContextYou are a teenager inside the novel's world. Use what you know from the text (the hack on the Moon, the malfunction, the company's refusal, how the feed shapes daily life) to make your situation believable.
AudienceA powerful corporate executive who does not know you and has no reason to care.
PurposeTo persuade. Think about what would actually move a corporation: reputation, fairness, public trust, consequences.
RegisterFormal and controlled. Polite but firm. Feed slang (“unit”, “meg”, “null”) does not suit this reader; if you use it at all, use it once and on purpose.
ConventionsAddress and date, a formal salutation, a clear opening that states why you are writing, paragraphs that build your case, a closing request, and a formal sign-off.

Task 3 (30 marks)✕
Timing recommendation: 35 minutes
  • Global context – Globalization and sustainability
  • This task is assessed according to criteria B (Organizing), C (Producing text) and D (Using language).
  • This task is worth 30 marks (B – 10 marks, C – 10 marks, D – 10 marks).
You live in the world of M. T. Anderson's Feed, where forests have been cleared to build air factories, the oceans are dying and people are developing lesions on their skin. Create a letter to the President of the United States urging the government to take action to protect the environment and the health of its citizens.
(Approximately 350 words)

Before you write: context and register

ContextYou are a citizen inside the novel's world. Draw on details from the text (air factories, lesions, the dead ocean, the government's response) to ground your argument.
AudienceThe head of a government that has so far played down the crisis. Your reader holds power and may be defensive.
PurposeTo urge action. Name the problem, show its human cost, and propose what the government should do.
RegisterFormal and respectful, with urgency. Avoid insults or sarcasm; a reasoned tone is more persuasive to an authority. Keep feed slang out unless you use it deliberately to show who you are.
ConventionsAddress and date, a formal salutation (for example, “Dear Mr President”), a clear statement of purpose, structured paragraphs, a specific call to action, and a formal sign-off.
